Research project · Machine Learning · Healthcare

A Resistome-Driven Ensemble Framework for Antimicrobial Resistance Phenotype Prediction

Safaet Jaman (Saddam Hossen)

🎯 Problem background

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is a critical global health crisis — the WHO estimates it will cause 10 million deaths annually by 2050 if unchecked. Bacteria acquire resistance genes (the resistome) through mutation and horizontal gene transfer, making resistance traits complex and difficult to predict from clinical observation alone.

Current standard-of-care diagnostic tests (e.g., disk diffusion, broth microdilution) can take 24–72 hours, delaying appropriate treatment and contributing to worse patient outcomes. Rapid, accurate in-silico prediction of resistance phenotypes from genomic or clinical features would allow clinicians to act sooner — and reduce the misuse of broad-spectrum antibiotics that accelerates resistance spread.

❓ Research question

Can a resistome-informed ensemble machine learning framework predict the antibiotic resistance phenotype (susceptible vs. resistant) of bacterial isolates more accurately than individual classifiers — using features derived from genomic resistance gene profiles alongside clinical and microbiological metadata?

⚙️ Methodology

1

Resistome profiles + clinical metadata

5
Evaluation
Cross-validation, precision, recall, F1, AUC-ROC
  • Binary classification target: antibiotic-susceptible vs. antibiotic-resistant phenotype
  • Resistome feature representation: presence/absence binary matrix of known resistance genes (ARGs) identified via tools such as ResFinder or CARD
  • Clinical covariates: species, isolation site, sample year, patient demographics (where available)
  • Class imbalance handling: SMOTE oversampling and class-weight adjustment
  • Base classifiers: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost
  • Ensemble strategy: stacking with meta-learner + soft-vote combination
  • Interpretability: SHAP feature importance analysis on top ensemble model
  • Cross-validation: stratified K-fold (K=5 or 10) to prevent data leakage

Bad habits — removal plan
Remove Late-night phone scrolling
Why harmful: Delays sleep, overstimulates the brain, destroys your sleep schedule.
Replace with: Quran recitation, dua, or a physical book at night.
Daily action: Put phone in another room at 9:30 PM.
Emergency rule: If you feel the urge, open Quran app instead — 1 page only.
Remove Irregular sleep
Why harmful: Disrupts hormones, lowers IQ temporarily, ruins mood and productivity.
Replace with: Fixed wake time — non-negotiable, even if slept late.
Daily action: Set 2 alarms. Put them across the room.
Emergency rule: Wake on time anyway. Sleep less tonight, better tomorrow.
Reduce Wasting time online
Why harmful: Time blindness — 10 minutes becomes 3 hours without noticing.
Replace with: Structured internet time: 15 min blocks with a timer.
Daily action: Use app timers (YouTube limit: 30 min/day).
Emergency rule: Close the app. Open your task list. Do 5 minutes of study first.
Reduce Delaying study
Why harmful: Procrastination compounds — tomorrow becomes never.
Replace with: 2-minute rule: start the task for just 2 minutes. You'll continue.
Daily action: Open your textbook or laptop immediately after breakfast.
Emergency rule: Study just 1 page, 1 problem, or 1 paragraph. That counts.
Reduce Starting many things, finishing nothing
Why harmful: Creates anxiety, no real progress, low self-confidence.
Replace with: Choose 1 big task per day. Finish before starting another.
Daily action: Write only 3 tasks. Mark them in order of priority.
Emergency rule: If overwhelmed, pick the smallest task and do only that.
Manage Overthinking
Why harmful: Paralyses action. 90% of worries never happen.
Replace with: Write the worry down, then write 1 action you can take.
Daily action: After Fajr, recite "Hasbunallah wa ni'mal wakeel" 10 times with intention.
Emergency rule: Talk to Allah in dua about the specific worry. Then act.
Good habits — building system
Use the "tiny start" rule: make each habit so small it feels embarrassing NOT to do it.
Build Sleeping early
Tiny start: Sleep 30 min earlier than last night. Put phone away first. Start with 11:30 PM this week.
Build Fajr prayer
Tiny start: Set alarm 5 min before Fajr adhan. Wudu is already prepared (do it before sleeping). Sunnah: sleep with wudu.
Build Quran daily
Tiny start: 1 ayah after Fajr. Increase to 1 page, then half juz when ready. Consistency beats quantity.
Build Daily academic study
Tiny start: 1 Pomodoro (25 min) at the same time daily. Same chair, same time = automatic habit.
Build AI/ML learning
Tiny start: Watch 1 short video or read 5 pages of a textbook daily. Build a Kaggle or GitHub habit.
Build Daily planning
Tiny start: Write 3 tasks on paper every morning. Takes 2 minutes. Changes your entire day.
Build Weekly review
Every Friday after Jumu'ah: 20 min review. Celebrate what worked. Adjust what didn't.

15 professional lifestyle rules
01
Prayer before productivity. Your 5 daily prayers are non-negotiable. Everything else adjusts around them — not the other way.
02
Wake time is fixed. You may go to sleep late on some days. You may never wake up late. The fixed wake time is your anchor.
03
Plan before acting. Spend 5 minutes every morning writing 3 tasks. A planned hour beats 3 unplanned hours.
04
One task at a time. Close every tab not related to your current task. Multitasking is a myth — it just does everything badly.
05
The phone serves you. Use it as a tool for 15-min blocks. It is not entertainment between prayer times.
06
Never miss twice. Miss a prayer? Make it up immediately. Miss a study session? Do it the same evening. One miss is an accident. Two is the beginning of a habit.
07
Done is better than perfect. Apply to the job even if your resume isn't perfect. Submit the assignment even if imperfect. Action beats paralysis.
08
Learn something every single day. Even on rest days — 10 minutes of reading, 1 hadith, or 1 YouTube lecture counts. Knowledge compounds.
09
Track your career like a project. Use a spreadsheet: jobs applied, status, follow-up date. Treat your career search like a professional, not a prayer.
10
Rest is part of the plan. Friday afternoon, 20-min Qaylulah, family time — these are not failures. They are fuel for the next sprint.
11
Your environment is your productivity. Clean desk = clear mind. Phone in another room = focused study. Supportive friends = motivated you.
12
Speak with intention. Every conversation, every message, every application — choose your words carefully. A professional communicates clearly and kindly.
13
Review weekly, adjust monthly. The routine is not a prison. It's a living system. Review every Friday. Adjust every month based on what's working.
14
Failure is data, not identity. A bad week is information: something in the system needs adjusting. It does not mean you are a failure.
15
Tawakkul + action. Make dua. Then work as if the result depends entirely on your effort. That is the Islamic formula for success.
Consistency system
How to restart after failure
Take a breath. Say Bismillah. Start the very next small task — don't try to "catch up" all at once. One prayer, one page, one task. That's the restart.
Avoid all-or-nothing thinking
"I missed Fajr so my whole day is ruined" is a lie your brain tells you. Pray Fajr late (qada). Start studying at 2 PM. Do something. 50% of the plan done is infinitely better than 0%.
Track progress simply
Use this checklist daily. Use the weekly review every Friday. After 4 weeks, compare Week 1 vs Week 4. The growth will motivate you.
Halal rewards
After a good week: buy a book you wanted. Take a walk somewhere new. Make a special meal. Call a friend. These are the halal pleasures that sustain discipline without haram escapes.
Your 1% daily improvement
Each day, do 1 thing slightly better: sleep 15 min earlier, study 5 min longer, read 1 extra ayah, take the stairs. In 70 days, you'll be unrecognisable from where you started.
Final advice
You already have everything you need to begin. The goal is not a perfect routine — it is a better you than yesterday.

Start tonight: sleep 30 minutes earlier than usual. That is Week 1, Day 1.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "The most beloved deeds to Allah are those done consistently, even if they are small." (Bukhari & Muslim)

You are not trying to become a different person. You are uncovering the best version of who you already are — a Muslim, a student, a professional, and a person of discipline and sincerity.
